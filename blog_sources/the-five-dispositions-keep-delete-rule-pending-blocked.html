The Five Dispositions: Keep / Delete / Rule / Pending / Blocked

By the Buoy team · 6 min read

Decision first: every HubSpot property that feeds a number you take to your leadership team gets exactly one outcome. Keep it, delete it, turn it into a rule, mark it pending on a named person, or mark it blocked on a process decision. No “we’ll look at it later.” This is the bottom-up half of an audit, and it matters as much as agreeing on definitions.

Why “keep or delete” isn’t enough

Binary cleanup sounds decisive. It isn’t.

Keeping a field nobody owns just preserves the confusion. Deleting a field without knowing which number it fed can break a report your leadership team relies on. Most contested properties need something else: a rule, a person, or a decision.

And a field can be completely filled and still wrong. Filled isn’t the same as true.

The five outcomes

Outcome Meaning Use it when
Keep Needed, defined, owned It feeds a leadership number and leadership will defend its definition
Delete Noise It fakes a signal and no decision depends on it
Turn into a rule Meaning is clear, capture is wrong It should be calculated, picked from a list, or required at a step
Pending Waiting on a named person One owner needs to confirm the meaning, by a set date
Blocked Waiting on a process decision The field can’t be honest until leadership decides how the process works

Keep

Before you keep a field, answer four questions:

  1. Which leadership number does it feed?
  2. Who owns its definition?
  3. What does a correct value look like, not just a filled one?
  4. Would a leadership decision get worse if it were wrong?

Delete

Good candidates: old experiments nobody reports on, three versions of the same fact with different math, and freeform notes that compete with an owned property. If a field shows up in a leadership report, leadership signs off before it goes.

Turn into a rule

Use this when everyone agrees what the field means but people capture it inconsistently. A few examples:

Today As a rule
Forecast category picked by feel Category follows stage and evidence, with rare, visible overrides
Nurture as an open stage Closed-lost with a required date and note
Free-text “renewal date” A contract end date, kept separate from next billing date

That last one comes up often. A property called “renewal date” frequently holds the next billing date, and your renewal and churn numbers inherit the confusion.

Pending

Pending is not a parking lot. It names one person and a date. “TBD” doesn’t count.

Blocked

Blocked means the field can’t be honest until leadership makes a process call. Post-sales onboarding is a common example: sales usually has written exit criteria, onboarding rarely does. Until someone writes them, any “onboarding complete” field is a guess. Blocked surfaces that argument instead of hiding it.

How to apply them without fixing everything at once

  • Start with the short list of numbers you take to your leadership team.
  • Work only on the properties that feed those numbers.
  • Agree on each number’s definition first (the top-down half), then disposition its properties (the bottom-up half).
  • Review Pending and Blocked items in your normal leadership rhythm until they clear.
  • Add enforcement only after both halves are done.

FAQ

What are the five HubSpot property dispositions?

Keep, Delete, Turn into a rule, Pending (on a named person), and Blocked (on a process decision).

What’s the difference between Pending and Blocked?

Pending waits on one person to confirm a meaning. Blocked waits on leadership to decide how the process itself works.

Should we disposition every property in HubSpot?

No. Start with the properties that feed the numbers you take to your leadership team.

Is cleanup the main part of an audit?

It’s half. The other half is agreeing on definitions and process. Neither works without the other.
