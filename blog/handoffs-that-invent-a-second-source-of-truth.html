Handoffs That Invent a Second Source of Truth

By the Buoy team · 11 min read

Every handoff is a chance to invent a second source of truth. Sales celebrates Closed-Won. CS opens a kickoff sheet. Product provisions from a ticket. Finance books from a contract PDF. None of those motions are wrong—until the facts that leadership will defend live only in the handoff artifact and not in HubSpot (or an explicitly linked, owned system). CEOs, COOs, and CROs who ignore handoff design inherit three CRMs and one leadership deck that reconciles them. Trust the handoff model first; then trust the numbers.

By the Buoy team · buildwithbuoy.com · ~11 min read

What a handoff is at executive altitude

A handoff is any moment where ownership of the customer story moves—and the receiving team needs facts the sending team believes are already “done.” Classic ones:

  • Sales → CS (or Onboarding)
  • Quote / CPQ → Deal → Entitlement / Product
  • CS → Renewal / Expansion motion
  • Support ↔ CS ↔ Sales on risk and champions
  • Portco ops → PE reporting pack

The operating question is not “did we schedule a meeting?” It is: after the handoff, which object holds the commercial and process facts—and can leadership drill without a translator?

If the answer is “the Google Doc,” “the Slack canvas,” or “what the CSM remembers from kickoff,” you have invented a second source of truth. The CRM stays full. Decisions get worse.

Filled ≠ true. A Closed-Won with every required field completed still fails if CS re-enters seats, package, and success criteria in a parallel system that never writes back.

Why handoffs invent second truths (even on good teams)

It is rational under pressure:

  • Kickoff needs richer narrative than the deal layout allows
  • Product needs provisioning detail Sales never captured cleanly
  • CS tools feel faster for health and playbooks than HubSpot custom objects
  • Finance trusts the signed PDF more than the deal amount
  • PE asks for a portfolio template that does not match any portco’s fields

Speed creates shadow systems. Shadow systems become the real operating model. HubSpot becomes a museum of the win announcement.

The fix is blunt: handoffs only hold when the fields they carry are dispositioned against how your process is supposed to work—not when teams are nicer about emailing attachments.

The handoffs that break decisions most often

Handoff Facts leadership needs after Second-truth pattern
Sales → CS Package, seats, use case, champions, success criteria, commercial start Kickoff sheet becomes system of record; deal goes cold
Quote → Won SKU mix and amount Finance will recognize PDF ≠ deal fields; “we’ll fix in billing”
Won → Entitlement What was provisioned vs sold Seats edited in product/billing; CRM never updated
CS → Renewal Contract end + current entitlement + risk Health score and a spreadsheet calendar; no renewal deal
Expansion sell Same commercial vocabulary as original New deal invents ARR that NRR will not cosign
Risk escalation Champion loss, usage drop, open risk Slack thread; CRM risk field blank or “Other”

Each pattern is a foundation incident dressed as collaboration.

The fifteen-minute handoff honesty check

Use this in a CRO/COO/Head of CS working session. Sample fifteen Closed-Won deals from the last two quarters that should have reached CS.

For each, score Yes / No / Unknown:

  1. Can you see package / seats / commercial start on the HubSpot record (or linked owned source) without opening a kickoff doc?
  2. Did CS re-key any of those facts into another system as the working copy?
  3. Is there a written rule for which system wins when deal and CS workspace disagree?
  4. Is renewal date present where leadership expects it—or only in the handoff artifact?
  5. If you asked a new CSM to run the account from HubSpot alone for a week, would they be flying blind?

If (2) is often Yes and (3) is No, you do not have a training problem. You have an undispositioned handoff. Dashboards that roll up those deals are decorative.

“We have a handoff process” vs “we have one source of truth”

Process theater looks like:

  • A checklist PDF nobody updates when the product catalog changes
  • A mandatory kickoff meeting with no field-level write-back
  • Required HubSpot properties filled with “see Notion”
  • A Zap that creates a CS project but does not sync commercial truth
  • Managers who “know the real package” in forecast and QBRs

One source of truth looks like:

  • Dispositioned material fields with object ownership
  • Handoff artifacts that reference CRM truth, not replace it
  • Conflict rules when quote, CRM, billing, and CS disagree—and visibility in-record when conflict exists
  • Renewal and expansion motions that read entitlement language Sales and Finance already own
  • Later (on the roadmap): in-record markers when handoff-critical fields drift or stay blank past the motion’s definition of good

Enforcement without disposition locks theater: every deal gets “Implementation notes: TBD” so the stage gate opens, and CS still builds the real plan elsewhere.

Disposition the handoff before you buy another tool

  1. Name the decisions the handoff feeds — time-to-value, expansion coverage, renewal Commit, NRR, support routing, PE “what did they buy?”
  2. Inventory material handoff fields on deal, company, line item/subscription, ticket—material means: if this lies or only lives in a sheet, an executive decision gets worse
  3. Disposition each one — Keep / Delete / Turn into a rule / Pending on a named person / Blocked on a process decision
  4. Assign ownership — who owns meaning at each stage; who is allowed to change seats after won; where renewal date lives
  5. Only then automate project creation, sync, or in-record honesty markers

Pending on “does CS or Sales own success criteria after Closed-Won?” is an executive conversation. RevOps should not silently pick a side in a workflow.

Operating posture for leaders

For CEOs and COOs

  • Treat parallel kickoff systems that rewrite commercial facts as foundation risk, not “CS tooling preference”
  • Demand that one mid-market customer be narratable from HubSpot + linked owned sources without Slack archaeology
  • Put commercial-loop handoffs on the same visual weight as pipeline hygiene in operating reviews

For CROs

  • Do not call the deal done at Closed-Won if handoff fields leadership needs are still Pending or Blocked
  • Align AEs on package language that CS and Finance will inherit—not nicknames that die at kickoff
  • Refuse expansion forecasts that ignore current entitlement invented in the CS workspace

For Heads of CS and Product (stakeholders)

  • Own the receiving definition of good: which fields must be true before you accept the account
  • Escalate missing contract dates—do not rebuild them quietly in a sheet
  • Cosign conflict rules with Sales and Finance; do not absorb second-truth work as heroics

Leaders decide meaning. RevOps runs the system. When handoffs are “owned by whoever is nicest in Slack,” the second source of truth wins by default.

A thirty-day shift from handoff theater to record truth

Week 1 — Map three handoffs only

Sales→CS, Won→Entitlement, CS→Renewal. Write what must be true in-record after each for the decisions you actually run.

Week 2 — Sample and list second truths

Fifteen deals. Every time a sheet, ticket, or Slack message held the working copy, log it. That list is the backlog—not a shame list.

Week 3 — Disposition the break fields

Keep / Delete / Rule / Pending / Blocked. Schedule the Blocked fights (catalog language, seat-change authority, renewal object ownership).

Week 4 — Change the motion

Kickoff starts from the CRM record. Handoff docs become views or links, not re-keys. Any in-record markers come after the model—not as a substitute for deciding who owns the fact.

You will not collapse every tool in thirty days. You will stop pretending the win announcement was the foundation.

What good looks like

Operating evidence beats slogans:

  • Material handoff fields dispositioned with owners
  • CS and Product can run from CRM-true commercial facts (or explicitly linked systems)
  • Conflict rules when systems disagree—and visibility when they do
  • Renewal and expansion using the same vocabulary as the original won
  • Leadership team and PE questions that survive drill-down without a war room
  • Later, in-record markers (on the roadmap) when handoff-critical facts drift

If you cannot point to those, another kickoff template will not create one source of truth. It will format the second one more nicely.

FAQ

What is a second source of truth in a Sales-to-CS handoff?

It is any working copy of commercial or process facts—seats, package, success criteria, renewal date—that lives outside the owned CRM (or linked system) and is what the team actually operates from. HubSpot becomes an announcement log; decisions follow the shadow system.

Are kickoff documents always bad?

No. They fail when they replace dispositioned CRM truth instead of narrating it. Reference the record; do not re-key the commercial spine into a doc that never writes back.

Who should own handoff field definitions?

Leaders cosign meaning (CRO, COO, Head of CS, Finance as needed). RevOps implements. If RevOps alone owns “what good looks like at handoff,” definitions soft-fail the first hard disagreement.

What comes after the handoff fields are defined?

The Audit defines which handoff-critical fields must be true and who owns them. Flagging those fields in the record when they go blank, contradict, or drift is on the roadmap—so risks show while there’s still time to act, not only when onboarding is already late.

What should a CEO ask instead of “improve our handoff process”?

Ask which fields must be true after Sales→CS and Won→Entitlement, who owns conflict when systems disagree, and whether a customer can be narrated without a translator. Process follows that foundation—not the other way around.
