The gap between what your CRM can hold and what it actually knows

Most CRMs aren't short on fields. They're short on anyone enforcing what those fields are supposed to mean — on Deals, on Contacts, on Companies, on every object your team touches.

The cost compounds quietly. Poor data quality is estimated to cost companies 15–25% of annual revenue, according to longtime data-quality researcher Thomas Redman. Only one in five B2B sales orgs hit their forecast within 5% of target, per Xactly's 2024 benchmark report — and Salesforce's own research found 46% of sales professionals say data quality is actively hurting their sales.

Not every check is the same kind of check

Here's what that looks like on a deal record — the same engine runs the same way on every other object.

Blank isn't a judgment call

Close date and economic buyer are empty. That's caught instantly, no interpretation needed — the moment the field changes.

Filled in isn't the same as true

"Next step: followed up, will call again" isn't blank — a simple rule would wave it through. Checked against this client's actual stage-exit criteria, it's flagged: no real commitment, no timeline, nothing to hold the deal to.

Two systems can both look right and still disagree

A rep moves a deal to Solution Presentation. This client's forecasting agent independently reads the same deal as still in Initial Discovery. Neither field is blank, neither throws an error — HubSpot has no concept of checking whether two systems' answers to the same question actually agree. Buoy does, the moment either one changes.

Why "Buoy"

A buoy doesn't stop the ship. It marks a hazard in real time, while there's still room to turn — which is exactly the job this does inside a CRM: not blocking someone from saving a bad record, just flagging it clearly while there's still time to fix it. It's directional, too — the same marker works whether you're looking at a deal, a contact, or a support ticket.
